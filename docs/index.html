An AI copywriting skill that works like an ad agency

Good ads rarely come from one person writing one good sentence. A planner reframes the brief, a creative director reviews it, several creative teams write a lot of lines, the CD cuts them down more than once, and the client picks. This Claude Code skill runs that workflow with one AI agent per role and hands you a headline, two alternates and a record of how they got there.

WORKFLOW

Ten steps, a different agent for each role

Roles never see each other's reasoning, only the files the previous step left behind. Teams never pick their own lines, and the CD judges lines with the team names hidden. In Turnbull & Wheeler's study of London agencies, only two of 24 steps produced ideas; the rest were about agreeing on the problem and checking the work. This skill spends most of its steps the same way.

  1. Client brief 01-client-brief.md

    The inputs, written down as given, with no interpretation.

  2. Raw material 02-raw.md

    Product facts, what real people wrote, and competitor copy, collected verbatim with sources.

  3. Planner 03-creative-brief.md

    Reframes the task as "what this ad must say" by changing the audience, changing the kind of problem, or finding the empty spot competitors leave. Points to the one scene or quote the reframe rests on.

  4. Creative surgery 04-surgery.md

    The CD reviews the brief before any team sees it. If it goes back, the planner revises once.

  5. Three creative teams, in sequence 05-team-A/B/C.md

    Each team opens 3 to 5 routes and writes 33 lines. Later teams are told which routes are already taken and look elsewhere.

  6. CD review, round 1 06-cd-review-1.md

    Criteria first, then a keep or kill on every line, with a short reason for each kill. 3 to 5 routes survive with feedback.

  7. Rework and defense 07-team-A/B/C.md

    Teams rewrite their surviving routes and may defend one killed line. Approved customer quotes enter the pool verbatim.

  8. CD review, round 2 08-cd-review-2.md

    Rules on each defense and shortlists about five lines for the client.

  9. Client review 09-client-review.md

    Fact and advertising-law check first, then one final line and two alternates. Unverified facts are flagged for the client to confirm.

  10. Handoff 10-final.md · 10-process-check.md

    The path each chosen line took, and a table checking that every step ran as the agency workflow expects.

FROM CD ROUND 1

Every line gets a verdict and a reason

Real verdicts from the demo run, with English glosses. The CD does not pick by taste or by counting pros and cons; it checks each line against criteria written down before reading. Length is never a reason.

Requires Claude Code and Python 3 (the helper scripts use only the standard library). Raw material collection uses a web search tool when one is available and falls back to your own material. Start a new session and copy-masters appears in the skill list. Update with git pull.

USAGE

Tell it what, for whom, and where it runs

Call it with the slash command or just ask for ad copy or headlines. If a required input is missing it asks first; it never guesses the mode, channel or target. Field names are Korean; the values can be written in any language, and the copy comes out in Korean.

Output

One file per step under copy-runs/<brand>/<date>-<topic>/ in your working directory. At the end you get the final line and two alternates, the planner's reframe, line counts per step, any lines the fact check caught, and the facts the client still needs to confirm.

Time and cost

The demo run used 13 sub-agents and took about 20 minutes, about 1.04M sub-agent tokens in total. It suits work where you want to follow the process or where fact checking matters more than speed.

LIMITS

No evidence it beats a single prompt

  • Compared head to head with a single prompt given the same raw material, the skill's final lines won 0.37 of the time on average. Treat its output as about as good as, or worse than, one well-written prompt.
  • The reason to use it is the process. You can follow each step, and the brief check and client review catch factual errors a single prompt repeated across runs, such as unsupported claims or edited customer quotes.
  • The CD and the client are the same model. Research suggests model judges match people poorly on originality and surprise, and re-running the same pool can change the final line.
  • It produces headlines. Visuals, subheads and body copy are out of scope.
  • Its only material is web sources and what you give it. Reframes that come from product demos, focus groups or a remark in a meeting room are out of reach.

The name copy-masters comes from versions 1 to 4, which imitated the methods of famous copywriters (Ogilvy, Bernbach, Taniyama, Jung Chul). Version 5 dropped that design and follows the agency workflow instead. The design draws on production records of 25 well-known campaigns and on Turnbull & Wheeler's research on London agencies.
